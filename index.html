Our understanding

The goal is for MoBot to find its docking station on its own and drive into it accurately. To do this, an ArUco tag (a black-and-white square marker, like a simple QR code) is placed on the dock, and the robot uses its camera to locate it.

1. Camera calibration. The camera is calibrated first so we know its focal length and lens distortion. Without this, the robot can't accurately judge distances from the image.

2. Tag detection. Each camera frame is processed (using OpenCV's ArUco library) to find the tag's four corners and read its ID, so the robot knows it's looking at the right marker.

3. Pose estimation. Using the corners, the known tag size and the calibration, we calculate where the tag is relative to the camera: how far away it is, how far left or right, and what angle it's facing.

4. Navigation. The robot turns and drives to reduce the sideways offset and angle error, using a controller (e.g. PID) to keep the motion smooth instead of jerky.

5. Docking. As it gets close, the robot slows down and makes fine adjustments so it lines up straight with the dock, stopping once it reaches the target distance.
